Article 1
This law applies to all civil matters arising from contracts, obligations, property rights, and family relations, but does not apply to commercial or administrative matters regulated by special laws.

Article 2
A contract is a mutual agreement resulting in an obligation between two parties. A contract shall be valid only if both parties have legal capacity and its subject matter is lawful and possible and certain or capable of being determined.

Article 3
Legal capacity is the ability of a person to acquire rights and bear obligations, which is completed by reaching the age of majority, which is eighteen years, and a person loses his legal capacity due to insanity, idiocy, or imbecility.

Article 4
Every person shall exercise his rights and fulfill his obligations in good faith. Good faith requires honesty in dealings and the absence of intent to harm others, and it is not permissible to use one's right with the intent to harm others.

Article 5
A contract of sale is an agreement that entails the transfer of ownership of a specific thing from the seller to the buyer for a monetary price. The seller is obligated to deliver the sold item in the agreed condition and guarantee it against latent defects that appear after delivery, while the buyer is obligated to pay the price and take possession of the item.

Article 6
If the sold item perishes before its delivery due to an irresistible force not attributable to the seller, the contract shall be rescinded and the buyer shall return the price. If the perishing results from the seller's act or fault, he is obligated to compensate the buyer for all damages incurred.

Article 7
A lease contract is an agreement that entails granting the tenant the right to use a specific property for a certain period in exchange for rent. The landlord is obligated to deliver the leased property in a condition suitable for the intended use.

Article 8
A mortgage is a real right established on property as security for a debt, and the secured party may request at the debtor's insolvency the sale of the mortgaged property by public auction and recovery of his debt from its proceeds.

Article 9
The obligation of means is which the debtor undertakes to exert the care of a person accustomed in performing the obligation without guaranteeing any specific result, and the debtor is discharged if he proves that he exerted the required care and did not achieve the result due to a cause not attributable to him.

Article 10
The obligation of result is which the debtor undertakes to achieve a specific result. The debtor shall only be discharged upon proving that the failure to achieve the result was due to an external cause not attributable to him, such as force majeure or an unforeseen event.

Article 11
A commercial partnership is a contract under which two or more persons agree to contribute money or work for a joint project and share the profits and losses arising therefrom. The partners are jointly liable for the debts of the commercial partnership.

Article 12
A contract of mandate is where one party undertakes to perform a legal act on behalf of the other party, and the agent must act within the scope of the mandate and provide an account of its execution.

Article 13
Prescription is the acquisition of a right or the lapse of an obligation by the passage of time. The period for civil claims prescription is fifteen years unless otherwise provided by law.

Article 14
Good faith is a general principle governing all legal transactions, requiring the contracting party to act honestly and not intend to deceive the other party or conceal material information that would affect their satisfaction.

Article 15
If damage occurs to a third party due to his fault, he is obligated to make amends. The compensation shall include restoring the case to its original state or paying compensation equal to the value of the damage.
